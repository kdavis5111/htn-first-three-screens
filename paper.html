Wildfires destroy more than 600 structures near Spokane, Washington developing

Why it mattersMore than 600 homes, businesses and other buildings near Spokane have burned, and evacuation orders were still in place as of Sunday. Outlets differ on the scale of the evacuation: two report about 5,000 homes, one reports about 60,000 people. The fires were 0% contained as of Sunday, August 2.

The facts ●3 verified○1 reported◐1 disputed

  • verifiedWildfires around Spokane, Washington - the Old Trails, Autumn Lane and Fairview fires, together called the Spokane Area Fires - have destroyed more than 600 homes, businesses and other buildings.— CBS News, PBS NewsHour, Washington Times and KHQ (citing InciWeb / California Interagency Incident Management Team 7) all report roughly 600-640 structures destroyed
  • reportedThe fires had burned about 5,390 acres (8.4 square miles) as of Sunday morning, August 2, per InciWeb; CBS News reported the burned area at about 12 square miles by Sunday evening.— InciWeb figure via KHQ for the Sunday-morning count; CBS News for the Sunday-evening count. The fire was growing between the two snapshots, so the difference reflects timing, not conflicting counts.
  • disputedEvacuation figures differ by outlet: PBS NewsHour and the Washington Times reported about 5,000 homes evacuated, while CBS News reported about 60,000 people evacuated, quoting Spokane Mayor Lisa Brown.— PBS NewsHour and Washington Times: ~5,000 homes; CBS News: ~60,000 people. The two figures use different units and no outlet reconciled them.
  • verifiedWashington Governor Bob Ferguson declared a state of emergency on Saturday, August 1.— Reported independently by CBS News and the Washington Times
  • verifiedAs of Sunday, August 2, the fires were 0% contained and no deaths had been confirmed. Spokane Mayor Lisa Brown said officials were still assessing the potential for lives lost.— CBS News, PBS NewsHour and Washington Times all report no confirmed deaths and no containment as of Sunday; CBS quotes Mayor Brown directly
